# NOTES — Snooze Button Roast

## Final entry (v2)

> My alarm clock went off at 6 AM on a Saturday like it had a court date and I was its alibi.

## Revision history

- **v1:** "My alarm clock rang at 6 AM on a Saturday like it had a court date and I was its alibi."
  The court-date image was fresh (the clock as a defendant, the owner as
  its unwilling alibi) and the punchline lands in the last words, but
  "rang" is flat and the line reads like a plain statement.
- **v2 (final):** "rang" → "went off", which is the idiom people actually
  use for alarms and doubles as a joke (the clock "went off" like it lost
  its temper). The rest is unchanged: the twist still pays off in the
  final clause.

## Discarded alternatives

- "My alarm clock has never heard of weekends — it rings at 6 AM on Saturday like HR approved the overtime."
  Decent office image, but "never heard of weekends" is a well-worn setup
  and the punchline ("HR approved the overtime") explains itself instead
  of surprising.
- "At 6 AM on a Saturday, my alarm clock screamed like a rooster that failed its audition for a farm."
  Concrete image, but two metaphors (scream + rooster + audition) crowd
  one breath, and the rooster angle is the oldest alarm-clock joke there is.
- "I hit snooze so many times my alarm clock filed a restraining order."
  Funny premise, but it roasts the snooze button / the sleeper, not the
  clock ringing at 6 AM on a Saturday — off-brief for criterion 1.
- "Whoever invented the alarm clock clearly never met a Saturday."
  Directly one of the tired angles the rules warn against ("who invented
  it") — rejected on sight.
- "I hate mornings, and my alarm clock knows it — 6 AM, Saturday, full volume."
  Pure "I hate mornings" grumbling, the other cliché the rules name.
  No turn, no punchline.
